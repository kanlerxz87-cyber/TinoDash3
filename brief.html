Attorney & CPA Briefing Sheet

Future inheritance of a California single-family home (Property A) alongside an already-owned rent-controlled multi-unit building in San Francisco (Property B). Decision window: approximately 2040–2050.

No path selected

1. The matter, in one paragraph

Property A is expected to be inherited by two siblings. Property B is already owned and remains owned in every scenario. The question is not “sell or keep” but which of the eight modelled paths — immediate sale, sale into a staged Treasury program, 12-month equity entry, hold as rental, occupy long term, rent-then-sell, occupy-then-sell, or sell half and rent half — is legally available, and at what cost. A §1031 exchange is a separate legal question, not one of the eight.

Nothing in this sheet identifies the properties or the household. Do not add identifying detail when annotating.

2. Three tax systems, kept apart

  • Estate tax — measured on the whole estate plus prior taxable gifts, once, at death. California imposes no estate or inheritance tax.
  • Income-tax basis — generally fair market value at date of death, reset by the inheritance, not by the deceased's purchase price.
  • Property-tax assessment — a change in ownership resets the base year value unless a narrow exclusion applies; growth is then capped.

The most common error is letting one system's answer decide another. Keep separate workpapers.

3. Attorney — please confirm or rule out

  • Trust or will authority, successor trustee powers, title, beneficiary shares, and whether sale or lease needs consent or court approval.
  • Administration calendar: notices, creditor period, accountings, distribution, and the deadline that actually binds the household.
  • Prop. 19 family-home exclusion: whether Property A can be the heir's principal residence, whether the value limit is met, and the exemption and claim filings with their windows.
  • Whether a §1031 exchange is available at all — it is not available for a personal residence, and the decision must be made before listing.
  • Property B: unit coverage, lease compliance, rent-increase licensing, and the effect of any proposed local limit that is not yet enacted.

4. CPA — please quantify in ranges

  • Independent date-of-death appraisal; basis; whether alternate valuation or special-use valuation is available and advisable; consistent-basis reporting.
  • Whole-estate liquidity: exclusion available, prior taxable gifts, portability election, Form 706 filing position, and the payment calendar.
  • Federal and California gain on each candidate sale year, including the §121 exclusion, nonqualified use, depreciation taken or allowable, and unrecaptured §1250 gain.
  • Rental depreciation and recapture schedules for both properties; passive-loss position; improvement records.
  • Estimated-tax consequences of a sale in a single year, including the California 30/40/0/30 instalment pattern.

5. Facts that are still missing

  • Trust, title, debt, lien, payoff, insurance and occupancy records.
  • Date-of-death valuation and the assessor's rate area and bill for Property A.
  • Independent condition report, five-year capital plan, written market-rent estimate, insurance quotes, utility and solar records.
  • Property B lease file, unit status, rent history, registration and expense records.
  • Whole-estate inventory and any gift-tax returns filed in prior years.

Twelve gates are listed at questions.html — none is closed.

6. Transaction-cost questions, priced

  • County documentary transfer tax is published at $0.55 per $500 or fractional portion of value, excluding liens already of record; certain cities add their own rate.
  • Recording fees are published per document and per page, with stated statutory add-ons.
  • Municipality-specific high-value transfer taxes must be confirmed for the actual city at the actual date.
  • Property A's rate area and therefore its exact bill remain blank pending the private bill.

7. Investment policy — request

  • Before proceeds arrive: reserve for estate tax, income tax and repairs; Treasury maturity ladder; how long proceeds are held; the phase-in schedule into equities; allocation; rebalancing; reporting; and the conditions that would override the policy.
  • Staging reduces crash-entry exposure and adds reinvestment risk. The published model reports the constant ladder yield that staging would have needed to match immediate investment, per scenario, rather than assuming one.
  • No return forecast is provided anywhere in this analysis.

8. Calendar to open immediately

  • At the event: preserve valuation, condition, occupancy, title, debt and liquidity evidence before anything is moved or improved.
  • Months 1–3: confirm authority, insurance continuity, and whether the family-home exclusion is even possible.
  • Federal return: generally due nine months after death, with an extension available only if requested on time.
  • Exclusion and exemption filings: have their own windows; a missed filing can forfeit an assessment benefit permanently.
  • Record: decision ______ · date ______ · reviewer ______ · next review ______.

9. What is being asked of you

Provide, in writing: (a) which facts are materially missing and who owns obtaining each; (b) every deadline with its trigger date and owner; (c) which of the eight modelled paths are legally available and which are foreclosed, and whether a §1031 exchange is available at all; (d) tax treatment as a range, given private facts; (e) anything that would invalidate the household's pre-agreed selection criteria. Do not place protected facts in a shared or public channel.
