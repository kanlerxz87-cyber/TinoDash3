Attorney & CPA Briefing Sheet

Private, evidence-gated planning for a future inherited California home (Property A) alongside an already-owned multi-unit home (Property B).

No recommendation yet

1. Matter / asset distinction

  • Property A: expected future inheritance; exact identity and figures withheld.
  • Property B: already owned; it remains in every Property A path and needs separate lease / local-rule review.
  • Decision options: Year-0 sale; rent; live; rent-then-sell; live-then-sell; sale → 3M–2Y Treasury ladder for 24–36 months → phased equities.

2. Immediate position

  • Do not rank paths yet. Basis, tax-rate area, valuation, rent, debt, capex, insurance, leases, and whole-estate facts are unverified.
  • Model a sale in the same tax year as inheritance first, using date-of-death FMV—not an old owner’s cost—as the starting basis question [1].
  • Prop. 19 is not assumed for a rental, short-term, or merely intended occupancy plan [2].

3. Attorney requests

  • Confirm trust authority, title, beneficiaries, successor trustee powers, creditor / administration process, and whether a sale/lease requires consents.
  • Validate Prop. 19 family-home eligibility, primary-residence facts, county process, one-year exemption timing, and claim timing [2].
  • Review Property B leases, unit coverage, registration/inventory, rent-increase license, notices, and current local rules [7].
  • Decide whether a §1031 exchange is wanted before listing / receiving proceeds; confirm investment-use eligibility and structure [5].

4. CPA / tax requests

  • Obtain independent date-of-death valuation; establish income-tax basis; screen alternate valuation and consistent-basis reporting [1], [3].
  • Prepare whole-estate liquidity and exposure analysis: federal exclusion, prior taxable gifts, marital facts, Form 706, portability, and payment calendar [3].
  • Run same-year and later-sale federal/California gain sensitivities; California taxes capital gains as ordinary income and has return-level additional-tax rules [4].
  • Rebuild rental depreciation, passive, improvements, and recapture schedules for both properties [6].

5. Private evidence to obtain

  • Trust/title/debt/lien/payoff/insurance records; whole-estate inventory and gift history.
  • Date-of-death valuation; Property A tax bill / TRA / assessment history; private closing estimate.
  • Independent condition report, five-year capital plan, written market-rent estimate, insurance quotes, and utilities/solar documents.
  • Property B lease file, occupancy/unit status, rent history, rent-board compliance, expense and depreciation records.

6. Property-tax and sale-cost gates

  • Keep estate tax, income-tax basis, and property-tax assessment in separate workpapers.
  • Property A TRA / published rate: blank pending private bill/assessor verification.
  • County transfer tax is published as $0.55 per $500 or fraction; recording schedule is published separately. Municipality-specific amounts must be confirmed privately [8].
  • Carry Prop. 19 as a future-date formula; do not carry today’s indexed allowance into a later transfer year [2].

7. Investment-policy request

  • Before proceeds arrive, document reserve for estate/tax/repairs; Treasury maturity ladder; 24–36 month hold; 12-month equity phase-in; asset allocation; rebalancing; tax reporting; override conditions; owner/date/review cycle.
  • Staging softens immediate crash-entry exposure but adds reinvestment risk and can miss upside. Current Treasury yields are a snapshot, not a forecast [9].

8. Calendar / decision record

  • At event: preserve valuation, condition, title, occupancy, debts, and liquidity evidence.
  • Generally 9 months: Form 706/tax/portability review; extension rules require counsel [3].
  • If qualifying Prop. 19 path: move-in/exemption timing and claim window need immediate calendar control [2].
  • Record: decision = ______; date = ______; evidence version = ______; next review = ______.

9. Requested professional conclusion

Provide a written list of (a) facts that are legally/tax materially missing; (b) deadlines and who owns each; (c) which sale, rent, live, and mixed paths are legally available; (d) tax treatment ranges based on private facts; (e) anything that would make the household’s pre-agreed selection criteria change. Do not place protected facts in a public collaboration channel.
