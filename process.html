Transfer process · current-law snapshot

Start with the date-of-death file, not a rent-versus-sell guess.

A trust distribution, income-tax basis, California property-tax assessment, federal estate tax, and rental compliance are different workflows. The timeline below puts irreversible deadlines before optional investment or marketing decisions.

Rule for the file

Record the decision and date.

For every choice, write the evidence reviewed, decision owner, date, conditions, and next review date. A future reader should know why—not just what—was chosen.

Deadline timeline

From pre-event planning through annual review

Deadlines depend on facts, filing status, and future law. This page shows current official starting points, not legal instructions for a specific estate.

Recheck at event
0

Now — build a private evidence room

Create a private inventory; preserve trust, title, mortgage, insurance, lease, utility, improvement, tax, and gift records outside this public repository. Ask for a written valuation plan and independent property/rent/condition work.

Private inventory checklist
DOD

Date of death / change-in-ownership event

Capture condition, valuation engagement, title/trust documents, occupancy facts, liabilities, and estate liquidity. For inherited property, IRS guidance generally uses date-of-death fair market value for basis; California BOE guidance treats an inheritance as a change in ownership unless an exclusion applies.

IRS Pub. 559 · CA BOE assessment guide
30

First 30 days — model a same-tax-year sale first

Get a written date-of-death valuation and private preliminary closing statement. Compare probable sale price with inherited basis; separately list transfer/recording charges, debt payoff, estate liquidity, and repairs. Do not assume a same-year sale has no gain or no tax.

Official recorder guidance
9m

Generally within 9 months — estate return / tax and portability review

The current Form 706 instructions state a nine-month filing and estate-tax due date, subject to extension rules. Ask immediately about prior taxable gifts, whole-estate value, liquidity, valuation elections, and portability. Do not wait for a property decision to ask.

IRS Form 706 instructions
1y

Within 1 year — Prop. 19 occupancy/exemption gate, if pursuing it

For the current California family-home exclusion, the transferee must meet the primary-residence condition and file for the homeowners’ or disabled veterans’ exemption within one year for relief from the transfer date. Do not claim this path for a rental or short-term occupancy plan.

CA BOE Prop. 19
3y

Within 3 years / before third-party transfer — Prop. 19 claim window

Current BOE summary guidance says the parent-child claim must be filed within three years of death/transfer or before a third-party transfer, whichever comes first. An assessor-specific exception may apply after a supplemental or escape assessment notice; ask counsel/assessor.

CA BOE Prop. 19 fact sheet
45/180

If a §1031 exchange is genuinely wanted — before selling

Current IRS guidance limits §1031 to qualifying real property held for investment or business, not a personal home. Deferred exchange identification and receipt deadlines can be 45 and 180 days. Engage qualified tax and exchange counsel before a sale contract or receipt of proceeds.

IRS Pub. 544
annual

Each year — recheck evidence, law, and extreme scenarios

Review the investment policy, tax-law monitor, insurance, rent rules, lease status, capital plan, source dates, and scenario outcomes. Record whether the recommendation moved and why.

California family-home reassessment check

Carry the Prop. 19 allowance forward as a formula—not today’s number

For a qualifying family home, current BOE materials describe a value limit based on the transferor’s factored base-year value plus an amount adjusted on a schedule. The currently published amount is date-specific. A future model should pull the then-current BOE amount at the event date.

Current formula
Qualifying family-home threshold = factored base-year value at transfer + then-current indexed allowance.
Current published allowance
$1,044,586 for qualifying transfers from 16 Feb 2025 through 15 Feb 2027. Do not carry this dollar amount into a future transfer year.
Required facts
Transferor’s qualifying principal-residence facts; transferee’s primary-residence/occupancy facts; exemption filing; claim timing; market value; factored base-year value; and current BOE guidance.
Model setting today
Blank / not claimed. It only becomes active after the household commits to long-term occupancy and the factual/legal requirements are validated.

BOE indexed-allowance release · BOE Prop. 19 overview

Do not use an occupancy story to get a tax answer

Claim this exclusion only if the heir will actually occupy long term.

The current rule is not a strategy for a rental or quick-sale path. The owner must be prepared to establish and continue a primary residence, complete the homeowners’ or disabled veterans’ exemption within the applicable deadline, and file the required claim.

  • Validate that the transferor’s home was eligible.
  • Validate transferee relationship and primary-residence facts.
  • Get the county assessor’s form and process at the event date.
  • Record move-in and exemption filing dates privately.
  • Recheck indexed allowance and any legislative change.

Property-tax rate area

Property A’s exact tax rate is intentionally blank.

A postal area does not uniquely establish a tax-rate area. No address or parcel number is placed in this public site and no public property lookup was performed. The private model must read the published rate attached to the property’s private tax bill / assessor record.

Prop. 13 model guardrail

Do not project taxes as a flat percentage of market value.

California BOE explains that assessed value and market value can diverge materially. The statewide basic rate is 1% plus rates for local voter-approved indebtedness; assessment growth is subject to rules and can differ during a Prop. 8 decline-in-value period.
