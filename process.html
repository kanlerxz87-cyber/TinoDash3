Transfer process · current-law snapshot

Start with the date-of-death file, not a rent-versus-sell guess.

A trust distribution, an income-tax basis, a California property-tax assessment, a federal estate return and a tenant-law file are five different workflows with five different deadlines. This page puts the irreversible ones first and shows the published arithmetic behind the numbers.

Rule for the file

Record the decision and the date.

For every choice, write the evidence reviewed, who decided, the date, the conditions, and what would reverse it. A future reader should know why, not just what.

One year — occupy the home and file the exemption, or give up the family-home exclusionhard gate

Prop. 19 requires the property to become the transferee's principal residence within one year of the transfer, and requires a homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption filing within that year. Miss it and the exclusion does not apply as of the transfer date.

3y

The claim for the Prop. 19 exclusion must be filed within three years of the purchase or transfer, or prior to a transfer to a third party, or before the transferee no longer occupies the residence — whichever comes first. Selling early therefore shortens the claim window.

Law monitor

Step by step

What actually happens between the death and the first dollar invested

  1. Confirm who has authorityRead the trust, any amendments, the deed and successor-trustee powers. Authority controls who can list, lease, borrow, claim and sign.
  2. Establish the date-of-death valueIndependent appraisal with retained comparables. This single number drives basis, gain, estate reporting and the same-year sale test.
  3. Determine what actually passes and to whomSpecific bequest, residue, fractional shares, or a trust that continues. This decides who pays the tax and who holds title.
  4. Measure the whole estateGross estate, deductions, prior taxable gifts, marital transfers and liquidity needs. A single house cannot answer the federal question.
  5. Record the change in ownership with the countyDeed or trust transfer recorded, Preliminary Change of Ownership Report filed, assessor notified. Recording fees and any transfer tax are paid here.
  6. Watch for the supplemental or escape assessmentThe assessor will issue a new assessed value. Check the rate area, the value and any exclusion claimed; a claim may be timely within six months of that notice.
  1. Decide the occupancy question honestlyMove in within the year and file the exemption, or accept that the family-home exclusion is gone. There is no partial version of this.
  2. Price the carryProperty tax at the new assessed value, insurance at the actual occupancy, water, power, upkeep, and — if rented — management and compliance.
  3. Get an independent rent opinionWritten, with comparables, days on market and concessions. Without it, the rental path is a floor, not an estimate.
  4. Fund the five-year capital planScope, bids, permits and contingency before the first tenant or the listing. Deferred work is the most common surprise in this decision.
  5. Settle, reserve, then investHold back tax and repair reserves before executing the ladder and the phase-in written in the policy.
  6. Write down the decision and its review dateOwner, evidence version, date, dissent, and the condition that would reopen it.

Two ways these get confused.

First: a low property-tax bill is not a low income tax. A home assessed at a decades-old base year value can still produce a large taxable gain on sale. Second: an estate that owes no federal tax can still need a Form 706, because a portability election requires a timely return regardless of size.

California family-home reassessment

Carry the Prop. 19 allowance as a formula, never as a number

For a qualifying family home, the excludable amount is the transferor's factored base year value at transfer plus an allowance that the Board of Equalization adjusts every two years. The currently published allowance covers transfers from 16 February 2025 through 15 February 2027.

Formula
New taxable value = transferor's taxable value + (market value at transfer − transferor's taxable value − current allowance), floored at zero.
Current allowance
$1,044,586. Do not carry this into a 2040–2050 transfer; the next adjustment has not been published.
Occupancy test
The home must become the transferee's principal residence within one year of the transfer.
Exemption test
A homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption must be filed within one year of the transfer, and the exclusion ends when eligibility ends.
Claim window
Within three years of the transfer, before a third-party transfer, or before the transferee stops occupying — whichever is earliest.
What it is not
It is not an income-tax benefit, it does not reduce the federal estate, and it does not apply to rental or investment use.

BOE allowance release · BOE Prop. 19 · R&TC §63.2

A worked shape, not a household number

Why the allowance rarely protects a long-held California home

Suppose the transferor's taxable value is a small fraction of current value — typical after decades of 2% caps. Even with the allowance added, the market value is usually far above the protected amount, and only the excess is added to the base. That is still a very large increase in annual property tax, but it is not the same as reassessing to full market value, and the difference is worth six figures over time.

Do not dress a rental up as a residence

Claim the exclusion only if the heir will genuinely occupy

The statute ties the exclusion to actual principal residence, and ends it when eligibility ends. A short move-in followed by renting out is exactly the pattern that triggers reassessment and, at the extreme, penalty exposure. Occupancy is an intent decision that has to be made before the tax decision, not after.

Why the rate band is a band

The statewide basic rate is 1%, plus rates for locally voter-approved bonded indebtedness. Direct charges and parcel taxes are not part of the 1% and are added separately on the bill. A parcel's rate area fixes the combination, and a postal code does not.

Why the growth is not fixed

The annual factor is the change in the California CPI, capped at 2%. When CPI runs above 2%, the cap binds; when it runs below, the factor is lower. Over 25 years that difference is material, which is why two variants are shown.

Why market value still matters

Under Prop. 8 mechanics the assessor enrolls the lower of the factored base year value or market value. A sustained decline can temporarily reduce the bill, and the value can then rise faster than 2% until it returns to the factored base year value.

What is deliberately absent.

The actual rate area, any direct charges, any Mello-Roos special tax and any city parcel tax. All four are parcel-specific and private. Substitute the real figures in the private workbook and re-run the model.

Estate-level questions the house cannot answer

Inheritance tax: what exists, what does not, and what is unknown

Fact

California imposes no inheritance tax and no gift tax. The recipient owes California nothing merely for receiving the property.

Fact

There is no federal inheritance tax on the recipient either. The federal transfer tax is imposed on the estate, above the basic exclusion, on the whole taxable estate plus adjusted taxable gifts.

Fact

The federal exclusion is $15,000,000 for deaths in 2026 and $15,480,000 for deaths in 2027. It is indexed after that, and it is law, not a forecast.

Unknown

Whether this estate is near either threshold. That depends on the whole balance sheet, prior gifts, marital status and any deductions — none of which can be assessed from one property.

Not modelled

Any projective estimate of the 2040–2050 exclusion. The model shows today's published amounts and stops. Projecting a specific future exclusion would be a forecast disguised as a fact.

A useful rule: the exclusion has risen faster than house prices in most recent years, but it has also been cut by statute before. Treat it as a monitoring item, not a constant.

What to ask now

Five estate questions with deadlines attached

  • Has portability already been used, and by whom?
  • What prior taxable gifts exist, and are the returns complete?
  • Is there liquidity outside the house for the estate tax and administration?
  • Does the trust direct the outcome, or does a beneficiary choose?
  • Is the surviving spouse's exclusion at risk because no return will be filed?
The expensive silence

Portability needs a timely return — even with no tax due.

An estate below the filing threshold can still file a timely Form 706 to elect portability. If nobody does, the unused exclusion is simply lost. That is a deadline question, not a house question, and it belongs on the calendar in the first week.

Tax-rate area · published rate · projection method

How the property-tax number is built—and why the exact rate stays blank here

Santa Clara County is divided into more than 800 tax rate areas. Each parcel's bill combines a base 1% with voter-approved bonds and special assessments, so the composite rate varies by area. A postal area is not a tax-rate area, and the specific Property A rate is deliberately left blank to keep the property non-identifiable; it must be read from the private tax bill / assessor record.

Projection method (inputs blank)

  • Start from the private base-year / factored base-year value, not market value.
  • Apply the annual inflation adjustment within the Prop. 13 growth limit, then voter-approved debt-rate changes from elections and bonds.
  • Apply Prop. 8 only if a verified decline-in-value review establishes a lower factored base-year value.
  • For a qualifying family home, carry the Prop. 19 formula forward: factored base-year value + the then-current indexed allowance.
  • Projected dollar outputs are rounded to the nearest $10; percentages and rates are shown as published.
Rounding rule

Rounded outputs are not rounded inputs.

The nearest-$10 rule applies to displayed dollar results so the site cannot be traced to a source property. Inputs, rates, and the private tax bill keep their published precision; do not round a rate or basis to force anonymity.

Federal exclusion · projection · law monitor

The inheritance threshold is a moving, law-dependent number

The federal basic exclusion applies to the whole taxable estate plus prior taxable gifts, not to one home. The target inheritance window is roughly 2040–2050, so today's threshold is a monitoring reference, not an estimate for the future event. California imposes no state inheritance or gift tax.
