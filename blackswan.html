Black Swan · phased investing

Does staging soften crash-entry risk, and what does it cost if no crash comes?

A stylised test of parking sale proceeds in 3-month to 2-year Treasuries for 24 or 36 months, then buying stocks in equal monthly steps over 12 months. Results are per $1,000,000 of proceeds and scale linearly. The crash depths are not yet verified against an index provider; reinvestment risk is shown beside every result.

Reinvestment risk, next to the headline

Staging trades crash exposure for cash yield risk.

Cash earned 4.36% on the official 3-month Treasury rate (24 Nov 2026). If cash yields fall to 2.00%, the staged result in every regime below is lower. Use both rows before deciding.

What is not yet verified on this page

  1. The 1929-type and 2008-type index drops and recovery lengths are stylised and need checking against an index-provider series.
  2. The equity growth after recovery and the staging design are the owner's assumptions, not official figures.
  3. The results show a deterministic path, not a probability distribution. They do not capture ties between cash yields and stock crashes.
  4. The baseline anchors are official SSA and CBO projections, not forecasts. The CBO 2056 long-range debt path is only secondary-cited and is not used here (flag F-26 covers wording).
