Reference · plain language

Nine terms do most of the work.

Most of the confusion in this decision comes from four words being used as if they meant the same thing: value, basis, assessment and gain. They do not. This page keeps them apart.

Short version

Value ≠ basis ≠ assessment.

Market value is what a buyer would pay. Basis is the tax starting point. Assessment is what the county taxes. Gain is the difference between a sale price and basis, after costs.

Transfer and estate

Change in ownership
A transfer of a present interest in real property, including the beneficial use, substantially equal in value to a fee interest. Inheritance is a change in ownership unless an exclusion applies.
Date of death
The reference date for inherited basis, for the estate's valuation, and for every deadline in the process.
Alternate valuation date
An estate election that values assets six months after death (or on disposition, if earlier). It changes both the estate value and the heirs' basis.
Consistent basis reporting
The requirement that a recipient's basis in property from a decedent match the value finally determined for federal estate tax purposes, with statements from the executor.
Portability / DSUE
Deceased spousal unused exclusion. A timely estate return can pass a deceased spouse's unused federal exclusion to the survivor.
Trust administration
The process by which a successor trustee takes control of trust property, accounts to beneficiaries and distributes or holds assets. It is separate from probate but has its own duties and records.
PCOR
Preliminary Change of Ownership Report. Filed with the county recorder at transfer; a missing or incomplete form adds a fee and can delay the assessor's work.

Property tax

Base year value (BYV)
The full cash value set when a property changes ownership or new construction is completed. It resets the assessment track.
Factored base year value (FBYV)
The base year value adjusted each year by the California CPI change, capped at 2%. This is the ceiling on the assessed value.
Prop. 8 value
A temporary reduction when market value falls below the factored base year value. The assessor enrolls the lower of the two.
Tax rate area (TRA)
The geographic unit that fixes which voter-approved debt rates and charges apply to a parcel. A postal code does not identify one.
Supplemental assessment
A bill for the difference between the old and new assessed value for the part of the year after a change in ownership. It arrives separately from the annual bill.
Escape assessment
A corrective assessment for property that was underassessed in prior years, for example because a change in ownership was not reported.
Direct charges
Charges on the tax bill that are not part of the 1% rate: special districts, parcel taxes, bonds and service charges.
Prop. 19 exclusion
A narrow California exclusion for a qualifying family home that becomes the heir's principal residence, subject to a value limit, an exemption filing and a claim window.
Homeowners' exemption
A $7,000 reduction in assessed value for an owner-occupied principal residence. It must be claimed, and it is also a condition of the Prop. 19 exclusion.

Income tax

Basis
The tax starting value for measuring gain. For inherited property it is generally the fair market value at death, subject to elections and exceptions.
Adjusted basis
Basis after additions for improvements and subtractions for depreciation taken or allowable.
Realised versus recognised gain
Realised is the arithmetic difference on sale. Recognised is the part that is actually taxed after exclusions.
§121 exclusion
The federal home-sale exclusion: up to $250,000 single or $500,000 joint, after owning and using the home as a main home for 24 of the last 60 months, with a two-year look-back and depreciation recapture handled separately.
Nonqualified use
Periods after 2008 when the home was not used as a principal residence, which can reduce the excludable portion of the gain.
Depreciation
Annual deductions for the wearing out of rental property. Residential rental real property is generally depreciated straight-line over 27.5 years, on the building only, not the land.
Unrecaptured §1250 gain
The part of a gain attributable to depreciation on real property, taxed at a maximum 25% rate rather than the usual long-term capital-gain rate.
§1031 exchange
Deferral of gain when qualifying real property held for investment or business is exchanged for like-kind property. A personal residence does not qualify.
NIIT
Net investment income tax: 3.8% on the lesser of net investment income or the excess of MAGI over the filing-status threshold.
Income in respect of a decedent
Income the deceased had earned but not received. It is taxable to whoever receives it and does not get a basis step-up.

Investment and modelling

Sequence risk
The risk that the order of returns, especially in the first years, determines the outcome more than the average return does.
Reinvestment risk
The risk that cash maturing from a short-term ladder must be reinvested at a yield that is unknown today.
Treasury ladder
A series of short-maturity government securities that mature at intervals, providing scheduled liquidity rather than a single decision date.
Phase-in (dollar-cost averaging)
Moving cash into a market exposure gradually over a defined period. It reduces timing risk and creates a known cost in a rising market.
Break-even yield
In this model, the constant ladder yield that would have been needed for a staged entry to match immediate entry over the same horizon. It prices the trade-off.
Imputed rent
The housing value of living in a home you own instead of paying rent. It is a comparison item, not cash, and it must be estimated from real comparables.
Opportunity cost of carry
The investment return forgone on cash spent on taxes, insurance, utilities and upkeep while occupying a property.
Normalised unit
A starting value of 1.00 used so that no household figure ever enters the public model. Results are multiples of it.
Robustness count
How many designed scenario-and-horizon cells a path finishes in the top two. It is a count, not a probability and not a recommendation.
Definitions are simplified on purpose.

Each one omits exceptions that matter in a real filing. Where a definition drives a decision, the source register links to the operative rule, and the decision requires a licensed professional's confirmation.
