Household figures · rounded to $10

What the numbers say, what is still unknown, and what would change the answer.

Every household figure is rounded to the nearest $10 and labelled by status. Official rules are linked; household inputs stay “unverified” until checked against a source record. Property A is the inherited home; Property B is the multi-unit home already owned. Educational only — not tax, legal, or investment advice.

What would change the answer

Four facts decide most of this.

  1. Whether the heir will occupy Property A as a principal residence and file on time (Prop 19 saves about $10,450 a year in the modelled case).
  2. A written date-of-death value for Property A (not yet obtained).
  3. Total estate value versus the federal exclusion (other assets are unknown).
  4. Market rent, insurance, and the five-year capital plan for both homes (none verified).

Property A · inheritance mechanics

Prop 19: keep the lower assessment only by occupying the home

A transfer from parent to child normally reassesses the home unless the Prop 19 family-home rules are met. The BOE sets the exclusion amount, adjusted every two years.

Income tax on a sale

Basis and California capital gains

Inherited property is not purchased property, so its basis is set by the inheritance rules, not the original purchase price. The gain on a later sale is taxed as ordinary income in California.
