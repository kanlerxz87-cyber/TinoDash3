Suggested review sequence

What to resolve before choosing a path

This is a recommended order for collecting evidence and making the decision—not a recommendation to live in, rent, or sell. The public model is not ready to rank this household's options.

Evidence gates open
1 · Pre-commit before seeing outcomes

Agree the decision rules in writing.

Set the household's priorities, time horizon, required liquidity, risk tolerance, occupancy intent, and the conditions that would favor selling, renting, living, or a mixed path.

By role: decision-makers, with an independent investment adviser if desired.

Gate: dated rules are signed before household figures are entered into a private model. Decision rules.

2 · Establish estate facts

Build the private inventory and confirm the transfer.

Collect trust and title documents, beneficiary interests, date-of-death valuation evidence, debts, other estate assets, prior taxable gifts, any portability filing, and known deadlines. Keep all source documents in an encrypted workspace outside this repository.

By role: trustee, estate attorney, tax adviser, and qualified appraiser.

Gate: the attorney and CPA reconcile the inventory and unresolved elections. Review IRS Publication 559 and the IRS Form 706 instructions.

3 · Replace estimates with evidence

Obtain independent property and operating ranges.

Request a written valuation, market-rent comparables, official assessment and applicable rate records, current insurance indications, lease and occupancy review, utility history, and a five-year maintenance/capital plan. Verify any use, transfer-tax, or tenant-law conditions for the actual transfer date.

By role: appraiser, property professional, insurer, building specialist, and local counsel.

Gate: each input has a dated source, low/base/high range, or a visible “unverified” status. Use the private inventory checklist and missing-information plan.

4 · Rebuild comparable paths

Use matched dates, horizons, taxes, and cost boundaries.

Run same-year sale first, then pure and mixed paths at common horizons. Include basis, depreciation and recapture, transaction costs, debt, occupancy changes, carrying and capital costs, imputed rent, liquidity, Property B's continuing cash flow, and staged-investing reinvestment risk. Stress inflation, returns, and crash-entry timing as ranges, not forecasts.

By role: tax adviser and an independent model reviewer.

Gate: the same documented inputs reproduce the outputs, sensitivities, and distribution chart. Paths and ranges · Black Swan tests.

5 · Decide, document, and revisit

Require professional review before irreversible action.

Record the selected path, decision date, rationale, alternatives rejected, delegated authority, liquidity plan, and conditions that would change the answer. Ask counsel and tax professionals to review legal and tax issues; use a qualified investment professional for portfolio implementation.

Gate: written reviews and the decision log are complete. Re-run annually and after a material law, occupancy, insurance, rent, or market change.

Printable attorney & CPA brief · Deadlines and process timeline · Review suggestions before implementation.

Known versus unknown.

The public pages provide dated law references, a normalized comparison method, and the distinction between an inherited asset and a separately owned asset. They do not establish the trust's controlling terms, future law, actual estate size, tax basis, property condition or value, rent, insurance, debt, eligibility, or household preferences. A professional must validate these before a household-specific conclusion is reasonable.
