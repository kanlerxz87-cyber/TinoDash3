The five facts that can each reverse the ranking

Written in advance, these thresholds stop a preferred path from being reverse-engineered out of one projection later. Keep the filled-in version in the private workbook, not in this repository.

Blocked
  1. 1 · Date-of-death value and the basis it createsInherited basis is generally fair market value at death under IRS Pub. 559. Without a written valuation there is no gain calculation, no same-year sale test and no defensible tax number.
  2. 2 · Whether the heir will really live there — and for how longProperty-tax relief under Prop. 19 needs the home to become the heir's principal residence within one year, plus a homeowners' exemption filing. The §121 income-tax exclusion needs 24 months of use inside five years. Both fail quietly if the plan is a short stay.
  3. 3 · Property A's tax rate area and real sale costsA postal code is not a tax rate area. The county sets the rate from the parcel's rate area; the sale adds county transfer tax, any city conveyance tax and recording fees from published schedules.
  4. 4 · Real market rent, condition and a five-year capital planRental paths are bounded by verified rent, vacancy, insurance, tenant rules and deferred maintenance. An unverified rent assumption can flip the ranking on its own — the sensitivity table on the model page shows it doing exactly that.
  5. 5 · The whole estate, prior gifts and portabilityFederal estate tax is measured on the entire taxable estate plus adjusted taxable gifts, not on one house. The 2026 exclusion is $15,000,000; the 2027 figure is $15,480,000. A future death year uses future law.

The honest headline

Parking proceeds in 24–36 months of 3M–2Y Treasuries helps exactly when the entry year is a crash — and costs money otherwise.

In a 1929-style sequence, a 30-month ladder finishes at 1.359 against 0.588 for immediate equity entry at five years. In the irregular baseline at 25 years it finishes at 2.293 against 2.367. That is the trade: crash protection paid for with expected return.

Reinvestment risk, beside the headline

The ladder has to earn a rate that no longer exists.

For staging to match immediate investment over ten years in a crash sequence, the parked cash needs a constant yield the model reports for every cell. The 24 November 2026 par curve showed 3-month at 4.36% and 2-year at 4.63% — published facts, not promises about 2029 maturities.

A ≠ B

One house is inherited. The other is already owned.

Mixing them is the most common modelling error in this file. Property B is not an inheritance and must not be counted as one; it is the household's fallback housing and cash-flow base while Property A is decided.

Property A · inherited, roughly 2040–2050 by trust

California single-family home

  • Ownership changes at death: basis resets, and the county reassesses unless an exclusion applies.
  • Vacant or rented while owned by the trust → no family-home exclusion, no §121 eligibility, full reassessment.
  • Three decision families: sell, rent, live — plus mixed and staged variants of each.
  • Carrying cost while held: property tax at the new assessed value, insurance, water, electricity, upkeep, and (if rented) management and tenant-law compliance.
Property B · already owned, never inherited

Multi-unit building (Property B)

  • Existing rental income continues in every Property A path, so it is a constant, not a differentiator.
  • Owner-occupying part of B while renting the rest is a separate lawful-use and rent-ordinance question, not a Property A option.
  • Rent-increase limits are set annually by the Rent Board (1.6% from 1 Mar 2026 to 28 Feb 2027; 2.3% from 1 Mar 2027).
  • A proposed 2026 ordinance (File No. 261020) would cap combined banked and capital-improvement increases. It is not enacted and is excluded from every number here.

Inheritance, step by step

The fourteen steps, in the order they bind

Deadlines run from the date of death, not from the date the family feels ready. The short ones come first.

  1. Before death: write the policyInvestment policy, decision thresholds and the private evidence room exist before any money moves.
  2. Date of death: freeze the factsPhotograph condition, note occupancy, collect trust and title documents, engage the appraiser.
  3. Day 1–30: written valuationIndependent date-of-death appraisal and a preliminary closing estimate before any listing conversation.
  4. Day 1–30: model the same-tax-year sale firstThe cheapest tax outcome to test and the one most often skipped.
  5. Within 1 year: move in, or don'tProp. 19 requires the home to become the heir's principal residence within one year plus a homeowners' exemption filing.
  6. Within 9 months (extendable): estate returnForm 706 and the portability decision are on a calendar whether or not the house is decided.
  7. Within 3 years: exclusion claimClaim filed within three years of transfer or before a sale to a third party, whichever is earlier.
  8. Day 1–90: property tax, insurance, utilitiesBudget the new assessed value, not the deceased's bill.
  9. Month 1–12: rent, live or listOnly after valuation, condition, rent opinion and the policy are in hand.
  10. If renting: 5-year capital planWritten scope, timing and contingency before the first tenant.
  11. Before any sale: confirm exchange intentA §1031 decision cannot be made after proceeds arrive.
  12. At sale: settle, reserve, invest by policyHold back tax and repair reserves; then execute the written ladder and phase-in.
  13. Annually: recheck law and extremesSources, rates, insurance, rent rules and the extreme scenarios.
  14. Each decision: write it down with a dateWho chose, on what evidence, what would reverse it.

A deliberate limitation

There is no “best plan” button, because there is no best plan yet.

A projection built on an unverified basis, rent, condition and tenancy facts would produce confidence that the evidence does not support. What this site can do is show which missing item changes the answer, and who should supply it.
